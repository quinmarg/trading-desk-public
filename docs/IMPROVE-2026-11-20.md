# Improving all three models from the logged research (2026-11-20)

The ask: use the research logged for all three models to make them better. Paper mode is for learning and should take
more trades where the evidence does not say they lose. Live mode changes only on strong evidence. Not financial advice.

## 1. Declared before any number below was computed

Committed before the backtest ran. Changing a test after seeing its result would make it worthless.

**Data.** A fresh fetch of Yahoo's free 5-minute bars (about 60 days) and a year of daily bars, into a scratch
folder, not the desk's `data/`, for two universes: **pool** (the desk's 88-stock `autolist.POOL`) and **desk** (every
stock the desk signalled on from 2026-11-11 to 2026-11-20, the high-volatility names paper mode now scans).
**The pool decides every pass rule**; the desk universe is reported as a second view only, because it was chosen
from the stocks that signalled on 2026-11-18 and 11-19, which are inside the data. Sessions up to 2026-11-19; 2026-11-20 was still trading. The rule is the desk's paper rule (B0 in
`docs/MODELS.md`): 15-minute range, range-middle stop, 2R target, 0.2R chase, 10-minute window, both sides, the
200-day rule for shorts, every signal (no odds floor). **Costs** are $0.01 a share plus 5 basis points a side on
every number. **Ranges** are 95 % day-block bootstraps (whole days resampled, since signals on one day move
together).

| test | question | change if it passes (paper mode only) | pass rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Does the in-play screen hold back good signals, and does that differ by side? | Signals of side S that are not in play become **ready** | Not-in-play S averages at least in-play S **and** the low end of its 95 % range is above 0, with 100+ trades. Otherwise nothing changes. |

**Live mode** changes only if a test passes the stricter rule from `docs/MODELS.md` section 5 (the low end of the 95 %
range above 0 on held-out data). None of the tests above is held out, so live mode stays as it is.

**Models 2 and 3.** Their sealed tests are used up, so nothing is re-tuned on 2010–2026. Only fixes that make them
follow their written rules more faithfully or fill more realistically, each backed by the logs or the rule text.

## 2. A gap that is not a rule

A sleeping computer runs no scans. If the Mac sleeps from 9:30 (a closed lid on battery, for example), the desk misses
the first half hour of breakouts, and models 2 and 3 trade when it wakes instead of at 9:35. The desk now logs every
gap in entry hours on the Health tab's event log.

## 4. Models 2 and 3

Both followed their written rules. Their sealed tests are used up, so nothing is re-tuned. Two notes:

- **Combined SPY.** With SPY under its 200-day average and in a dip, the dip vote is in and the book can hold 2/3. A
  bull-only dip vote would hold 1/3, so the two readings differ on such days. Kept as written (`docs/MODELS.md`
  section 1).
- **Winter 3x.** When SPY closes just under its 200-day average after closing just over it, the rule goes to cash and
  pays a round trip. A band around the average would avoid that, but the research does not back one: the
  beat-the-S&P development tried 0 to 2 % bands, the banded finalists (F2, F3) did worse, and F1 was sealed without
  one. Adding one now would be tuning after the test. Not changed.
